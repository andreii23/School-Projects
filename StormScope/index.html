Understanding Typhoons

🌊

What is a Typhoon?

A typhoon is a mature tropical cyclone that develops in the northwestern part of the Pacific Ocean. It's characterized by strong winds, heavy rainfall, and storm surges.

📊

Typhoon Categories

Typhoons are classified by wind speed:

  • Tropical Depression (<39 mph)
  • Tropical Storm (39-73 mph)
  • Typhoon (74+ mph)
  • Super Typhoon (150+ mph)
⚡

Formation Process

Typhoons form over warm ocean waters (26.5°C+) when atmospheric conditions create low pressure systems that intensify into rotating storms.

🌍

Batangas & Typhoons

Batangas, located in the CALABARZON region, is frequently affected by typhoons from the Pacific. The province's coastal areas are particularly vulnerable to storm surges and flooding.

Typhoon Formation Timeline

1

Warm Ocean Waters

Sea surface temperature above 26.5°C provides energy

2

Atmospheric Instability

Rising warm air creates low pressure system

3

Rotation Begins

Coriolis effect causes counter-clockwise rotation

4

Eye Formation

Central eye develops with calm conditions

5

Mature Typhoon

Full development with maximum wind speeds

⚠️ Most Vulnerable Areas in Batangas

🏖️

Coastal Areas

Batangas City, Calatagan, Lian, Nasugbu, and other coastal municipalities are highly vulnerable to storm surges and strong winds.

🌊

Low-lying Areas

Areas near Taal Lake, rivers, and floodplains are at risk of severe flooding during heavy rainfall.

⛰️

Mountainous Regions

Areas along Tagaytay Ridge and other elevated regions are prone to landslides during heavy rains.

🏙️

Urban Centers

Lipa, Tanauan, and other urban areas face flooding risks due to inadequate drainage systems.

Safety Tips & Resources

01

Before the Typhoon

  • Secure outdoor furniture and objects
  • Stock up on food, water, and supplies
  • Charge all electronic devices
  • Review evacuation routes
  • Stay informed via weather updates
02

During the Typhoon

  • Stay indoors and away from windows
  • Avoid using electrical appliances
  • Keep emergency kit accessible
  • Monitor weather updates
  • Do not go outside until authorities say it's safe
03

After the Typhoon

  • Wait for official clearance before going outside
  • Check for structural damage
  • Avoid floodwaters and downed power lines
  • Use flashlights instead of candles
  • Contact family to confirm safety
